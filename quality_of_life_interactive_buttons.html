Why the OECD?
The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) is an international organization whose member countries work together on economic and social policy. Its Well-being Data Monitor brings together 80+ indicators covering material conditions, health, safety, education and skills, environment, life satisfaction, work-life balance, social connections and civic engagement.
The OECD framework is the foundation for many dimensions used here. This chart is a comparative dashboard synthesis—not an official OECD overall ranking.

What is solid: The OECD well-being framework is an established international framework with 80+ indicators. Health, life expectancy, education, environment, social support, life satisfaction and work-life balance are recognized comparative dimensions.

Important qualification: There is no single official “overall quality-of-life ranking” for these 20 countries. The 1–20 positions are a dashboard synthesis intended to make many dimensions visually comparable. Different indicators use different years, definitions and methodologies.

Best practice: Use the chart to see patterns, then tap the indicator/source buttons before drawing conclusions about a particular measure.

How to Read This Chart

1 = stronger relative position; 20 = weaker relative position. Colors are visual aids, not grades.

The U.S. row is highlighted for easy comparison across the 20 dimensions. What matters depends on which dimensions a person values.

Primary framework: OECD Well-being Data Monitor • OECD Current Well-being • OECD Better Life Index.
